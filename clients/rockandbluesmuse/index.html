01  —  Platform Analysis

Is It WordPress? What's the Stack?

Confirmed: WordPress — A Solid Foundation for a Content-Heavy Publication

Rock and Blues Muse runs on WordPress, evidenced by the standard URL date-based permalink structure (/2026/02/27/article-slug/), the WordPress-style author archive pages (/author/martine/), the category taxonomy structure, and the "Load More Posts" pagination pattern typical of WordPress theme builders. For a high-volume publisher posting daily, WordPress is the correct choice — the question isn't the CMS, it's whether SEO plugins, schema, and technical hygiene are keeping pace with the publishing volume.

Ceiling Assessment — Why This Site Can Become the Authoritative Source for Blues-Rock AI Queries

Rock and Blues Muse has something almost no site in this audit series has: a 4,379+ article content archive with daily publishing cadence and genuine journalistic access. The problem isn't content — it's that none of that content is structured for AI systems to extract, cite, and surface in answers. This is fundamentally a schema and architecture project layered on top of an already-thriving editorial operation.

The AIO jump from 42 → 93 (+51 points) is the largest opportunity in this audit, and the mechanism is straightforward: Article/NewsArticle schema across the archive (even retroactively applied via a bulk plugin update), MusicGroup and MusicAlbum schema on artist coverage and album reviews, Review schema with structured ratings, and Person schema establishing Martine Ehrenclou and contributing writers as verified authors. Once implemented, AI systems asked "who are the best emerging blues-rock guitarists" or "what's the best new blues album this year" have a structured, dated, attributed, citable source — built from content that already exists.

The www/non-www canonicalization issue should be resolved immediately — split indexing between domain variants can quietly suppress ranking potential across the entire archive, which is especially costly given the content volume at stake. Fixing this is a single technical change with archive-wide impact.

Given the strength of the existing content engine, 91+ is achievable within 60 days of schema implementation and technical cleanup — content creation is already solved; this is purely an infrastructure and structured-data project layered on top of a publication that's clearly already winning on the editorial side.

04  —  Recommended Actions

Top Priority Fixes

01

Resolve www / Non-www Canonicalization

Content was found indexed under both rockandbluesmuse.com and www.rockandbluesmuse.com. With 4,379+ articles in the archive, even a partial split in canonical authority compounds into a meaningful SEO loss across the entire site. A single 301 redirect rule and consistent canonical tag implementation resolves this site-wide in one technical change.

Fix This Week
02

Implement Article + NewsArticle Schema Across the Archive

This is the single highest-leverage action available given the content volume. With WordPress, this can be implemented via a schema plugin (Yoast, RankMath, or custom) applied as a template across all post types — meaning the entire 4,379+ article archive gains structured headline, author, datePublished, dateModified, and image markup in a single deployment, rather than requiring article-by-article work. This is the rare case where scale works in your favor.

AIO +25 pts
03

Add MusicGroup, MusicAlbum & Review Schema to Music Coverage

Album reviews and artist interview content should carry MusicGroup schema (artist name, genre, sameAs links to official artist sites/socials) and MusicAlbum schema (album name, release date, recordLabel) where applicable. Reviews should carry Review schema with itemReviewed and ratingValue where the publication uses a rating system. This makes Rock and Blues Muse directly citable when AI systems answer questions about specific artists, albums, or "best of" genre queries — converting editorial content into structured, machine-readable music journalism.

AIO + Genre Authority
04

Build Out Martine Ehrenclou's Author Bio & Add Person Schema

Martine's author archive page currently displays "This author has not yet filled in any details" — despite being the senior editor and publisher with 4,379+ bylines and a verified Muck Rack journalist profile. A complete bio (background, areas of expertise, notable interviews conducted, awards if applicable) combined with Person schema (jobTitle, worksFor, sameAs links to Muck Rack and social profiles) establishes the E-E-A-T foundation that both Google and AI systems weight heavily for editorial credibility — especially valuable given her role as the public face of a $4M media business.

E-E-A-T Critical
05

Build a "Best Of" / Genre-Authority FAQ & Hub Page

With this much editorial expertise in blues-rock music, the site should own genre-defining queries directly: "What is blues-rock?", "Who are the best blues-rock guitarists today?", "Best new blues albums 2026" — built as a dedicated, regularly-updated hub page with FAQPage schema. This converts genuine subject-matter expertise (already evident across thousands of articles) into a single, AI-citable authority page that aggregates and links to the deeper archive content.

AIO + Genre Hub
06

Standardize Headline & Internal Linking Patterns Going Forward

Current headlines are well-written but inconsistently structured (some lead with artist name, others with achievement). Establishing a consistent SEO title pattern — e.g., "[Artist Name] [Action]: '[Release Title]' [Context]" — compounds in value across future publishing volume. Additionally, with 4,379+ archive articles, a systematic internal linking strategy (linking new artist coverage back to past interviews/reviews of the same artist) would meaningfully strengthen topical authority clusters that are likely already present but under-leveraged.
