High

Title and description carry no keyword or location

The homepage title is “Adults Only Oceanfront Hotel | TSLHBC”. The acronym means nothing to searchers, and neither the title nor the description mentions Lauderdale-by-the-Sea or Fort Lauderdale.

High

Heavy, doubled-up page-builder stack

WPBakery and Elementor both load. The homepage fires 120 requests, 52 scripts, a 268 KB DOM and no lazy-loaded images, against 10 requests, no scripts and 29 KB on the concept.

High

Stale and leaking content

The footer reads © 2022. COVID-era protocols (masks, no housekeeping, fitness center closed) are still in the page HTML, along with raw [fluentform] and [forminator_form] shortcodes.

Medium

Image alt text missing on 6 of 11 images

The social share image is a 2016 upload. The viewport tag also sets user-scalable=no, which blocks pinch-zoom and fails accessibility checks.

Medium

Hotel schema is thin

A Hotel block exists, which is a real strength, but it has only name, address, image, logo, url, phone and price range. No geo, amenities, check-in times, ratings or sameAs.

Strength

Solid indexing basics and strong third-party presence

The site is indexable, has a canonical, OG and Twitter tags, a Yoast sitemap index (10 sitemaps), and consistent name, address and phone. Expedia lists a 9.0/10 guest rating.

The Elettro build standard

What every Elettro site ships with, and how it moves each score

  • Technical 95: clean indexable build, canonicals, XML sitemap, redirect map for every existing room URL, no render-blocking plugins, accessible viewport
  • On-Page 92: keyword and location titles and descriptions on every page, one H1 per page, full alt text, current OG images
  • Structured Data / AIO 95: complete Hotel schema (geo, amenities, check-in and out, rooms as HotelRoom, ratings, sameAs), FAQPage, BreadcrumbList, and AI-crawler-ready robots and llms.txt
  • Content 85: rewritten, current room, dining, wellness and area pages with answer-style FAQs, and all legacy COVID copy removed
  • Performance 95: no page-builder stack, lazy-loaded images, a target LCP under 2.5s on field data
  • Local & Authority 70: held down on purpose. Reviews, citations and backlinks are off-site work that a website build alone does not deliver. That is where an AIO retainer moves the number past 90.
Tech stack

What the live site is built on

  • CMS: WordPress, Hotel Xenia theme with child theme
  • Builders: WPBakery (js_composer) and Elementor, running side by side
  • SEO: Yoast (sitemaps and schema graph); robots.txt written by a separate “SEO Setup Plugin”
  • Forms: Contact Form 7 (PayPal add-on, redirect), Fluent Forms, Forminator
  • Other plugins: Photo Gallery (10Web), GTranslate, Popup Maker
  • Third-party scripts: Google Tag Manager, Crazy Egg, KPI Analyser review widget, KeyCDN push/analytics
  • Hosting hint: assets from GoDaddy’s CDN (img1.wsimg.com). Hosting provider is not confirmed.
Reservation engine

Roommaster, on Seekda’s booking module

  • Every “Book Now” leaves the site for s006079.officialbookings.com
  • The engine footer reads “Powered by roommaster”
  • It loads scripts from Seekda (static/cloud.seekda.com) and runs the “KBE” booking module
  • The booking path sits on a separate domain, so guests lose the hotel’s branding and its analytics continuity at the moment of purchase
  • The Elettro concept points all booking CTAs to the same engine, so the proposal’s “existing booking engine” scope holds

Method. The live site was inspected in Chrome on October 27, 2026, and schema was read from the rendered DOM. Composite = Technical 20%, On-Page 20%, Structured Data 20%, Content 15%, Performance 15%, Local 10%. The current score is a weighted judgment from on-site evidence, not ranking data, and no backlink or traffic data was used. Performance is a single warm lab run (LCP about 0.95 s), not field data. The estimated 90 is based on Elettro’s standard build and holds only if every deliverable above ships and is verified after launch; it is not a guarantee of rankings. 06 / Common questions

Clear answers
upfront.

A proposal should remove uncertainty before a project starts.

Will the website resemble The Betsy Hotel?

Yes in design direction: editorial presentation, sophisticated typography, immersive photography, considered content hierarchy, and a seamless guest journey. The final website will be an original Sea Lord design and will not copy The Betsy's protected images, brand elements, or code.

Do you handle design and development together?

Yes. Elettro produces the site from creative strategy and visual design through front-end development, content integration, technical SEO, launch, and ongoing support.

Will our existing booking engine keep working?

The live website presently links to an external Official Bookings system. We would assess its capabilities, then integrate the approved reservations flow without replacing your booking platform unless you request a separate migration.

What about SEO, mobile speed, and AI search?

Responsive layouts, semantic content, structured data where appropriate, crawl/indexation checks, local SEO foundations, performance-conscious assets, and answer-friendly content are part of the planned build. Rankings and search-engine outcomes are never guaranteed.

Will we own our website?

Our agreement would specify ownership of your approved custom website deliverables, content, source code, and accounts after payment. Licensed third-party components remain subject to their own terms.

Do you provide photography, video, and ongoing content?

Yes, Elettro produces visual content and offers ongoing marketing support. New shoots, major copywriting, ongoing campaigns, and third-party image licensing would be priced once the deliverables are defined.

Is the linked homepage the final proposed website?

No. It is a working creative study to demonstrate the direction, interactions, page structure, and potential guest experience. Photos from public hotel listings and clearly illustrative lifestyle images require client-supplied originals or permission prior to a commercial launch.
