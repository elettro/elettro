Why hire an advocate?

You shouldn't need a medical degree to protect your interests.

Even excellent healthcare teams operate under pressure. Missed communication, unclear instructions and disconnected follow-up can leave patients vulnerable. An independent advocate brings structure, preparation and persistent questions to the process.

☷

Understand medical information

Review what your clinicians shared, unpack unfamiliar terms and organize questions about your diagnosis, care plan and options.

☏

Communicate with care teams

With your written permission, participate in calls or meetings, help document concerns and request clear next steps.

◎

Prepare for appointments

Arrive with a focused question list, a medication overview and the facts your provider needs to know.

↗

Track tests and referrals

Keep an organized record of pending results, specialist referrals, follow-up instructions and unanswered questions.

♡

Support families in a crisis

Reduce confusion when a loved one is hospitalized and family members are trying to understand changing information.

✓

Strengthen discharge planning

Ask about warning signs, medications, equipment, home support and which clinician owns each next step.

The goal is not to pick fights with doctors. It's to make sure your questions are answered, your preferences are heard and important details get attention.

10 situations worth questioning

What gets overlooked when nobody knows what to ask?

These are illustrative scenarios, not allegations against any particular doctor or hospital. Some result from system complexity, time pressure or poor communication. Each one shows where a knowledgeable advocate adds value.

01The abnormal test result nobody explains+

A result posts in the portal, but you don't know whether anyone reviewed it or what follow-up is appropriate.

Your advocate asks

Who reviewed this finding? Does it change the care plan? What follow-up has been ordered, and when should we expect it?

02“You're being discharged” with no clear plan+

A patient heads home unsure how to take new medication or recognize signs of deterioration.

Your advocate asks

Which medications changed? What warning signs require urgent attention? Who is arranging follow-up, and has the patient understood the instructions?

03Two specialists give conflicting instructions+

One clinician recommends a change while another says something different, and nobody connects the two plans.

Your advocate asks

Who is coordinating the treatment plan? Have both specialists reviewed the same medication list and records?

04A medication list that doesn't add up+

The chart includes old prescriptions, new prescriptions and unclear dosing changes.

Your advocate asks

Has a clinician or pharmacist reconciled the current medication list? Which medicines should the patient actually take?

05A specialist referral disappears into a queue+

A referral is discussed but scheduling stalls, or the receiving practice never gets the records.

Your advocate asks

Was the referral transmitted and received? What is the recommended timing? Is there an alternate in-network option?

06Consent for a procedure nobody fully explains+

Forms arrive quickly and the patient still doesn't understand the purpose, risks, alternatives or recovery expectations.

Your advocate asks

Can the treating clinician explain the risks, benefits, reasonable alternatives and what happens if treatment is delayed?

07A patient keeps repeating their story+

Each new shift or office requests the same history while important details risk being lost in the handoff.

Your advocate asks

Does the current team have the latest history, test results and care plan? Which information needs to be carried forward?

08The family is kept out of the conversation+

A patient wants a trusted person involved but the appropriate authorizations and contacts are unclear.

Your advocate asks

What authorization or healthcare surrogate documentation is needed, and who is responsible for providing authorized family updates?

09Insurance blocks the next step+

A needed appointment, test or treatment gets delayed amid authorization requirements or a coverage denial.

Your advocate asks

What is the exact reason for the denial? What appeal, peer review or alternative pathway is available through the provider and insurer?

10Symptoms change, but nobody revisits the plan+

A patient reports a change, but communication between the patient and treating team remains incomplete.

Your advocate asks

Has the appropriate clinician been notified? What reassessment or triage guidance has the treating team provided? In an emergency, call 911.

Illustrative examples only. An advocate raises questions and follows up. Diagnosis, clinical decisions and treatment remain with the patient's authorized treating professionals.

Ask us directly ↗
What does a private patient advocate do?

An advocate helps you prepare for medical conversations, understand information provided by your clinicians, organize records, coordinate authorized communication and follow up on unresolved questions. An advocate does not replace your medical team.

Can Marie-Claude speak directly with my doctors or hospital?

Yes, with appropriate written patient authorization and within the agreed scope. A HIPAA authorization supports information sharing. A separate healthcare surrogate designation may be needed for someone to make healthcare decisions when the patient is unable to do so.

Will the hospital have to follow her recommendations?

No. The treating medical team retains responsibility for clinical decisions. Marie-Claude can ask informed questions, clarify options, document concerns and help you request appropriate explanations or escalation.

Does this include diagnosis, prescriptions or treatment?

No. This membership is for independent advocacy and care navigation. Clinical diagnosis, prescribing and treatment require a separate, appropriately supervised medical care arrangement.

Is someone available 24 hours a day?

The membership is designed to address time-sensitive advocacy needs, but specific contact channels, response windows, after-hours arrangements and backup coverage must be defined in your agreement. This is not a 24/7 medical response or emergency service.

What happens if my loved one is hospitalized?

With permission, an advocate can help consolidate records and questions, prepare for care-team discussions, understand discharge instructions and coordinate family communication with designated hospital contacts.

Can you help with insurance authorizations and denials?

Advocacy may include helping you understand insurer notices, identify missing documents and prepare questions for your provider or insurer. Coverage decisions and appeal outcomes are not guaranteed.

Is advocacy covered by health insurance?

This is offered as a privately paid membership. Ask your insurer about possible reimbursement or benefits. No insurance coverage or reimbursement is promised.

How much does the membership cost?

The proposed premium rate is $1,000 per month, equivalent to $12,000 across 12 months. Final services and billing terms are confirmed before enrollment in a written agreement.
