Room Mate Hotels: one brand system, hundreds of touchpoints

Room Mate wrote the guidelines. Elettro made them real. We prepared, proofed, sourced, printed and shipped the guest-facing pieces that carried the orange Room Mate identity through Grace New York, Waldorf Towers and Lord Balfour, from a door hanger to a beach chair.

The brief

A playful identity that had to survive the real world

Room Mate Hotels is a Madrid-founded boutique group with a distinctive voice: a hand-drawn orange logotype, plain-spoken bilingual copy and small bits of humour, such as a door hanger that says "Don't throw in the towel!" and a notepad that reads "My house is your house."

The brand manual defined the rules. The hotels still needed the physical pieces: thousands of cards, hangers, tags, vouchers and giveaways, each cut to a specific size, ink, stock and finish, and each delivered to a different property with its own address, language mix and approvers.

That is where we came in. Elettro took Room Mate's specifications and turned them into print-ready files, proofs, supplier quotes, approved production runs and shipments.

The system

Four rules that held every piece together

One orange. PANTONE 165 C on white or black, with a secondary palette of turquoise, magenta, indigo and cyan for signage families like the green "Come in" and red "Do not disturb" hangers.

One typeface. Futura Std for body and tags, paired with the hand-lettered Room Mate logotype.

Iconography. A small friendly vocabulary: the apple, the heart, the speech bubble, the rotary phone, the duster, the bolt and nut, the tap and the key.

Bilingual by default. English and Spanish sit together on almost every item, with the print spec (size, ink, stock, finish) written beneath each application.

How it ran

From guideline to guest room

SpecRead the manual's size, ink, stock and finish for each application.
PrepBuilt production artwork and prep documents for each hotel and language.
SourceQuoted printers and suppliers for cards, vinyl, acrylic, bags, pens, sugar and umbrellas.
ProofSent proofs for sign-off. Business cards alone went through up to six rounds per person.
ProduceApproved runs went to press under Elettro's supplier accounts.
ShipBlind-shipped to each property on the client's carrier account, with invoices per hotel.
